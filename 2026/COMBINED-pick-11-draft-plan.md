# Master Pick 11 Draft Plan — Premiership 2026

> **Use this file on draft night.** It combines and adjudicates the ChatGPT, Grok, and Claude Markdown plans in this repository.

## League lock-in

- ESPN, 12 teams, head-to-head points
- Draft slot: **11th**
- One starting quarterback; not superflex
- Confirm reception scoring, lineup slots, bonuses, and bench size before the draft
- Working assumption until confirmed: standard ESPN PPR-style lineup

## What the three files actually say

The three files are useful, but they are not three independent votes. The Grok note and ChatGPT plan share the same core strategy. Claude adds the most explicit ADP and injury research, but occasionally turns uncertain news into rules that are too rigid.

| Agreement | Master decision |
|---|---|
| RB becomes scarce quickly | Leave the opening four rounds with at least two playable RBs unless extraordinary WR value forces a deviation |
| One-QB is deep | Do not pay an early QB premium; draft one starter at value |
| Draft near the turn | Think in two-pick pairs and account for the 21-player wait after pick 14 |
| ESPN's queue influences opponents | Use tiers and ADP, not projected-points order |
| Kicker and D/ST are replaceable | Take them in the final two rounds |
| Late bench picks need upside | Favor contingent RBs and ascending WRs over low-ceiling veterans |

## Resolved disagreements

### 1. James Cook or Justin Jefferson at 1.11?

**Resolution:** Take any elite top-10 faller first. If the expected ten are gone, take **Justin Jefferson**. If both James Cook and Jefferson are available, Cook gets the slight nod because workhorse RB supply is thinner—but the difference is small enough that roster preference can break the tie.

Jefferson remains the normal-board target because the 2.02 RB group is deeper than the 2.02 WR group and his 2025 result already depressed his price.

### 2. Quarterback in Rounds 6–8 or Round 9?

**Resolution:** Use a tier trigger, not a fixed round.

- At picks 83/86, draft a QB only if a preferred starter such as Herbert, Burrow, Hurts, Maye, or another top-six option has fallen and the RB/WR/TE core is healthy.
- Otherwise wait for picks 107/110 and target Prescott, Mahomes, Purdy, Goff, Lawrence, or the best remaining QB1.
- Do not draft a second quarterback in a normal one-QB league unless the bench is unusually deep.

### 3. Is the middle tight-end tier dead?

**Resolution:** No, but it must be priced correctly.

- Bowers/McBride: take only at a fair Round 2/3 price.
- Loveland/Warren: legitimate Round 4/5 options after comparing the RB/WR tier.
- Pitts/Kraft/LaPorta/Andrews tier: Round 6–9 value, not a reason to reach.
- Draft one TE. Add a second only if the first is injured or the late value is exceptional.

### 4. Is Ashton Jeanty usable at 2.02?

**Resolution:** Only after healthier players in the same tier. His ankle outlook has improved and he avoided IR, but uncertainty remains.

At pick 14, prefer Chase Brown, Barkley, Achane, Hampton, or Walker when available. Jeanty becomes reasonable if they are gone and the latest practice report is positive. If drafted, add a healthy September RB and monitor Mike Washington Jr. later.

## Picks 35 and 38

Make these selections as a pair.

### If the roster has one RB and one WR

- Take the best remaining RB first: Breece Hall, Travis Etienne Jr., Kyren Williams, Javonte Williams, Jeremiyah Love, Quinshon Judkins, or the best healthy equivalent.
- Pair him with the best WR: Chris Olave, Zay Flowers, Garrett Wilson, Tetairoa McMillan, DeVonta Smith, Ladd McConkey, Emeka Egbuka, or the best remaining equivalent.
- If Bowers or McBride somehow falls to 35, the positional advantage can override the pairing.

### If the roster has two RBs

Prioritize two WRs, or one WR plus an appropriately priced elite/upper-tier TE.

### If the roster has two WRs

Take at least one RB—and normally two—at this turn. Do not let Zero RB continue accidentally.

### Tight-end exception

Colston Loveland at 38 is defensible if the preferred RB/WR tier has emptied. Tyler Warren is a later version of the same decision. Neither should be selected simply because the elite TEs are gone.

## Picks 107 and 110

- This is the preferred late-QB turn if no earlier value fell.
- After QB, select an upside RB or WR—not a kicker.
- Useful profiles include Kenny Gainwell, Blake Corum, Jordan Mason, Mike Washington Jr., Keaton Mitchell, Rachaad White, Jordan Addison, Josh Downs, and other players one role change from weekly relevance.

## Final rounds

1. Contingent RB with a clear promotion path
2. Boom/breakout WR
3. Injury-specific handcuff when the backup has a real role
4. D/ST in the penultimate round
5. Kicker in the final round

The earlier handcuff rule was too absolute. Handcuff your own fragile RB when the backup is cheap and clearly inherits the job. Otherwise, drafting another team's high-upside backup can create more championship upside.

## Draft-room execution

Before every turn:

1. List the best five available players by tier.
2. Check the two teams picking between your paired selections.
3. Draft first the player those teams are most likely to take.
4. Look ahead through the full **21-pick wait** after the second selection.
5. Use bye weeks only as a late-round tiebreaker.

Send a screenshot of available players and the current roster at each turn. The live pick should account for who actually fell, not merely follow this document's names.

## Final roster shape

For a typical 16-player ESPN roster:

- 1 QB
- 5–6 RB
- 5–6 WR
- 1 TE
- 1 D/ST
- 1 kicker

Use a second QB or TE only when the bench is deeper than expected, the starter carries material injury risk, or the value is exceptional.

## One-sentence plan

Take the best elite faller at 11, secure a featured back by 14, build RB/WR depth through the middle rounds, treat QB and TE as tier decisions instead of scheduled picks, and use the late rounds on players whose roles can grow.
